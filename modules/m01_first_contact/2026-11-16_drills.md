Great work on **Parts A and B (7/7 — 100%)**! You nailed regular verb conjugation, the `-ß` and `-t` stem rules, and *sein*/*haben*. Here are the 5 items to review:

- **#9 — `Wie` vs. `Was` for names**
  - **Your answer:** *Was ist dein Nachname?*
  - **Correction:** **Wie** ist dein Nachname?
  - **Rule:** In German, always use **Wie** (not *Was*) when asking for someone's name, phone number, or address.

- **#10 — Verb conjugation with formal `Sie` (`sprechen` — *spricht, sprach, hat gesprochen*)**
  - **Your answer:** *Welche Sprachen specht Sie?*
  - **Correction:** Welche Sprachen **sprechen** Sie?
  - **Rule:** Two things here: (1) don't drop the **r** in *sp**r**echen*, and (2) formal **Sie** always takes the **-en** ending (same as the infinitive), never `-t`. Your word order was spot-on!

- **#11 — Formal `Sie` verb ending & possessive (`Ihr` vs. `Sein`)**
  - **Your answer:** *Guten Tag! Wie buchstabiere Sie Sein Nachname?*
  - **Correction:** Guten Tag! Wie **buchstabieren** Sie **Ihren Nachnamen**?
  - **Rule:**
    1. With formal **Sie**, the verb ends in **-en** (*buchstabier**en** Sie*).
    2. Formal "your" is **Ihr** (capitalized), whereas *sein* means "his".
    3. *(Preview for Module 4)*: As the direct object of *buchstabieren*, `der Nachname, -n` takes the Accusative form *Ihren Nachnamen*.

- **#12 — Spelling (`Tschüss`) & vocabulary (`morgen` vs. `später`)**
  - **Your answer:** *Tchuss, Lukas! Bis spaete!*
  - **Correction:** **Tschüss**, Lukas! Bis **morgen**!
  - **Rule:**
    1. *Tschüss* is spelled with **Tsch-** and an umlaut **ü**.
    2. *morgen* = tomorrow (*Bis morgen!* = See you tomorrow!). *später* (with **ä** and **-r**) = later (*Bis später!* = See you later!).

- **#14 — Compound numbers ending in 1**
  - **Your answer:** *einsundzwanzig*
  - **Correction:** **einundzwanzig**
  - **Rule:** Standalone `1` is *eins*, but in compound numbers (21, 31, 41, ...), it drops the `-s` and becomes **ein-** (*einundzwanzig*, *einunddreißig*).
